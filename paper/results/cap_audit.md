## Budget homogeneity

Grading by STATUS alone is not sufficient. A table whose rows are all `partial`
is still confounded if some were given 600 s and others four hours -- the longer
run simply got further. That confound produced two retracted findings (the
shortest-path detour comparison and the routing-distinctness table), and neither
was caught by the status check. This section exists because of them.

| grouping | groups with mixed budgets | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| across principles, at one $N$ | none | safe |
| across $N$, for one principle | proportional: [600, 14400], utilitarian: [600, 14400] | **CONFOUNDED** -- do not pool fleet sizes for these principles |

Any table that pools rows across a confounded grouping must either restrict to a
common budget or report the budget per cell. The detour table did neither and was
withdrawn.

### Every routing leaves some community at exactly zero added noise

**SAFE**

Holds in 205/205 returned routings (77 of them non-optimal).
This is a property of the ROUTING, not of the objective: a `partial` or even a
`fallback` route is still a real route satisfying every hard constraint, so it
is legitimate evidence. Phrase as 'in every routing returned', not 'in every
optimal routing'.

### Utilitarian flies least over 45 dBA communities (N=16)

**PARTIALLY UNSAFE -- ordering between principles needs proven rows**

Status at N=16: equal=optimal, sufficientarian=optimal, prioritarian=optimal, utilitarian=partial, proportional=partial

Tier share is read off the routing, so each number is real. But an ORDERING
between principles compares optima, and Utilitarian and Proportional are not at
theirs. Their true optima could sit either side. The long-budget re-runs settle
this; until then the ordering is stated with the caveat already in the text.

### Ablation: three of five principles bind on nothing (N=8, 16, 24)

**SAFE for the three that do not bind; CONSERVATIVE for the two that do**

  * equal: on=optimal, off=optimal
  * sufficientarian: on=optimal, off=optimal
  * prioritarian: on=optimal, off=optimal
  * utilitarian: on=partial, off=optimal
  * proportional: on=partial, off=optimal

Equal, Sufficientarian and Prioritarian are `optimal` both on and off at
all three demands -- nine paired proven comparisons -- so 'identical
arrival, unchanged own-metric' is a proven statement, now at n=3.

Utilitarian and Proportional are `partial` when ON. Their incumbent
already beats the control, so 'it binds' holds a fortiori at any demand
where that is true; only the SIZE of the improvement is a lower bound.

  * Budgets are matched across demands; trends across N are readable.

Metric note: each principle is scored on its OWN objective. An earlier version
scored three of five on total added dB, which for Proportional inverted the
result (+45.3% substituted against +1.1% on its priced objective).

### Tractability ordering

**SAFE, but must be described as a property of the BUDGET, not of the problem**

This table is the one place where hitting the cap IS the measurement. It says
what a participant experiences at the deployed 600 s budget. It does NOT locate
the tractability wall, and the paper must not claim it does; the long-budget
sweep is what speaks to that.

### Machine load across sweeps

**CONFOUNDED -- median phase-1 ratio 3.10x across sweeps**

40 configurations were solved in more than one sweep. Phase-1 wall time is budget-independent, so its ratio between sweeps measures
contention alone: median 3.10x, max 23.1x.

Consequence: a 600 s row and a four-hour row of the same configuration
differ in BUDGET and in LOAD. Every retraction in this paper rests on such
a pair. The direction of a retraction survives (both changes favour the
long run, and each retraction WITHDRAWS a claimed effect rather than
asserting a new one), but no wall-clock figure is a single-process
measurement and the tractability ordering should be read as ordinal.
This is the one confound the audit can detect but not correct.

### Detour from shortest path -- WITHDRAWN from the paper

  * sufficientarian: optimal=8
  * prioritarian: optimal=8
  * utilitarian: optimal=2, partial=6

Detour is read off the routing, so every number is a real routing's detour.

incumbent, and the optimum might detour differently. State as 'the routings the
solver returns', not 'the optimal routings'.
