### The indifference floor, and why no between-condition number stands alone

The fleet-size study found that holding ONE cap-based condition fixed and moving only an
incidental parameter -- the horizon, which provably leaves its arrival optimum untouched --
still changes the routing that comes back, by **5.64 dB of peak added noise and 5 aircraft
of peak occupancy at N=16**. Most differences *between* conditions at a fixed setup are
smaller than that, and are therefore not evidence about the condition.

Two consequences for anyone extending this work:

- `compare.py` reports that floor beside every spread, and `figs/floor.pdf` plots the
  ratio. A spread is only a finding when the ratio exceeds 1.
- A floor measured over fewer settings is an UNDERESTIMATE, so both the figure and the
  table omit any fleet size whose floor is incomplete rather than report a number that
  will move.

### The merge, and why it matters

`analyze.load()` transparently replaces any row with a stronger run of the same
experiment, matched two ways: by key alias (`rescue|X` supersedes `X`) and by
configuration (`bind|equal|10|2.0` and `scale|equal|communities|2.0` are one experiment
under two plan names). Without it, tables silently compare a 600 s incumbent against a
four-hour optimum --- an error that produced two wrong conclusions before the merge
existed (see `claims_audit.md`, C1 and C2).

Two guards on the merge, both learned the hard way:
- `MERGE_EXEMPT = {"reps"}` --- that sweep measures run-to-run variance at a *fixed*
  budget, so its repetitions share a config by construction and merging collapses them
  onto one row.
- `load(raw=True)` --- used by the tractability table and figure, whose subject is wall
  time. A row of mixed budgets is not comparable.

## Source of the fairness definitions

Section II is written from **`D:\Downloads\Fairness theories in a nutshell.pdf`**, the
workshop's own definition sheet. It supplies, for each of the five principles, the
general statement, the assumption it makes, its reading for this dashboard, and a
literature reference. Those references are the ones in `refs.bib` under
"Distributive justice"; everything else in the bibliography is supporting literature
found separately.

Two things in that document did **not** carry into the paper as-is and the reasons are
recorded here:

- Its Prioritarian and Proportional readings name zones from a 9-zone model
  ("Zone 4 (Cumulative Burden / Sacrifice Zone)", etc.) that does not correspond to the
  25-tile grid the solver runs on. The paper therefore states the *criterion* from the
  document and selects tiles by it explicitly (see `plans.py: PROT_BURDEN`), rather than
  claiming a mapping that does not exist.
- Its Sufficientarian note points at Austin's municipal ordinance for a threshold. The
  paper cites it as the natural policy anchor but does **not** use it as the cap value,
  because the ordinance governs absolute level and the solver caps *added* level. That
  distinction is itself a result (Sec. IV-E).

To stop a solve that will not finish and keep its work: create
`results/checkpoints/<key>.STOP` (per cell) or `results/checkpoints/STOP_ALL` (every
worker now running). One `main_ctx().interrupt()` is delivered, the solver's own
`_best_so_far` path salvages the incumbent and replays it against every hard constraint,
and the row lands as `partial`. **Prefer the per-cell file** -- `STOP_ALL` also hits
workers from any other sweep sharing the machine, and it will interrupt a job that starts
while the sentinel is still on disk, which is how four cells once landed as
`phase1_stopped`.

`runner.py` is resumable: it skips any job whose key is already in the output jsonl, so
an interrupted sweep can simply be restarted. Rows written as `NO_OUTPUT` (a killed
worker) are deliberately **not** treated as done.

## Measurement conditions

- Solver: `solver_server_dB.py` at commit `f231cb9`, unmodified. Z3 4.16.0.
- Two budgets. **600 s** is the production default and is what §IV-H measures, because it
  is what a participant experiences. **Four hours** was given to every solve that failed
  to settle, and to the comparisons that would otherwise rest on an unproven answer;
  `worker.py` raises the server's own 30-minute ceiling to allow it.
- `wall_kill` sits above the Z3 budget and hard-kills a worker that overruns it. Seven
  jobs hit it: Z3 can ignore its own timeout inside a long simplex call, so those rows
  are recorded as `NO_OUTPUT` and treated as unsettled, never as infeasible.
- Sweeps ran 6-9 worker processes at a time on a 20-core Windows workstation, and **not
  all sweeps ran under the same load**. Phase 1 is budget-independent, so its wall time is
  a clean contention probe: across the 40 configurations solved in more than one sweep the
  median phase-1 ratio between sweeps is **3.10x** (max 23.1x), with the long-budget
  sweeps on the quieter machine. Every 600 s-vs-4 h comparison therefore moves two
  variables. `cap_audit.py` reports this; it cannot correct it. Timings are comparable
  **within** a table, not across sweeps, and the tractability ordering should be read as
  ordinal.
- `reps` runs serially (`par: 1`) because it measures run-to-run timing variance, which
  contention would destroy.

### Status vocabulary

`analyze.status_mark` is the single definition, and the distinctions are load-bearing:

| status | means | may support |
|---|---|---|
| `optimal` | proven best for its objective | anything |
| `partial` | objective applied, incumbent verified against every hard constraint, optimality unproven | directional claims where the incumbent already beats the comparison; NOT orderings or exact values |
| `fallback` | phase 2 produced nothing; the served route is phase 1's unweighted one | nothing about the principle |
| `unsettled` | phase-1 timeout, or worker killed --- feasibility never decided | nothing; **not** the same as infeasible |
| `infeasible` | proven no routing exists (`noise_cap`, `unreachable`, `structural`) | that no routing exists |

Anything read off the returned *routing* --- occupancy, peak dB, spread, tier share,
detour --- is a real property of a real constraint-satisfying route even when that route
is `partial`. Such claims are safe as statements about "the routing returned", and unsafe
only if phrased as statements about "the optimal routing".

An earlier version of this file said "ICRA allows 6, or 8 with over-length charges."
**That was wrong and was never checked.** The CFP says:

- **8 pages, hard**, for the complete paper *including* references and acknowledgements.
- Papers over the limit are **returned without review**. There is no fee-based extension.
- Deadline **15 September 2026, 11:59 PST** --- ten days from this writing.
- Review is **double-anonymous**; `main.tex` now carries an anonymous author block with
  the real one commented out for camera-ready.

### Where the length actually stands

Body text is ~10,200 words across five sections, plus abstract, seven floats and the
bibliography. At ~1,150 words per full text page in IEEEtran two-column, plus ~1.6 pages
of floats, ~0.35 for the title block, ~0.6 for references and ~0.35 for §III's equations,
that lands near **11-12 pages against a hard 8**. Note it went UP this round: disclosing
the machine-load confound and the unreported saturation sweeps cost ~500 words, which was
the right trade but does not help the deadline.

Trimming has taken it from 10,504 to ~9,300, and **two whole findings were withdrawn on
their merits** (below), which was worth more than any prose cut. What remains is
defensible content: reaching 8 pages means deleting results that are true.

This is now a journal paper. The honest options are:

1. **Move venue.** T-RO, *Transportation Research Part C* (where Gao et al. published the

With ten days to the deadline, option 1 or 2 is the lower-risk path.

## A note on tooling

`check_tex.py` exists because a shell heredoc silently collapsed `\begin` into a
backspace control character in `sec4_results.tex`. Braces balanced, refs resolved, and
the damage was invisible until the file was read by eye. The checker now scans for
control characters and `\begin`/`\end` imbalance. **Do not edit the `.tex` files through
shell heredocs or `python -c`** — both eat backslashes in this environment. Use an
editor, or a Python script written to a file first.
