Use the real solver
Address
Start it first with python solver_server_dB.py — it listens on port 5051. If it is not running, the participant view shows that no route was generated; it does not display the rough offline estimate as a result.
Extra time steps
Extra time steps let aircraft wait instead of all crossing at once, which lowers the loudest moment. Measured on this map: 0 extra gave a +6.99 dB peak, 1 extra gave +4.77 dB, and 2 extra changed nothing while costing far more. Leave this at 1. Going from 0 to 1 costs about 1.3× the solve time (38s to 51s, repeated measures); the step from 1 to 2 is the expensive one and was last timed at roughly 8×, on a single unrepeated run. At 3 the solver ran out its whole budget and returned no answer at all, which is why 2 is the maximum here.
Equally-good routings to list
Equal and Sufficientarian set the solver no goal to minimise — their rule is the dB limit itself — so every routing inside the limit follows the rule equally well, and the one on the map was settled by arrival time and then by whatever the solver happened to find first. This asks for that many of them so the group can see whether the choice mattered. 0 switches it off. Each extra routing costs one more search: about 5s on top of a 17.5s solve at Equal 10 dB. If the solver answers that only one routing fits, that is the strongest outcome — nothing was arbitrary.
Show the quietest of them
Judge quietest by
Ranking happens here, not in the solver: these routings already tie on the group's rule and on arrival time, so putting the quietest on the map trades nothing away. It is the quietest of the few listed, not a proven minimum, and the panel on the map says so. Changing either of these clears the map, because the ranking is applied when a route is generated — press Run again, which is instant for a set-up already solved.
Give up aftermin
How long each of the solver's two phases may search. If the solver has a route that applies the selected principle when time runs out, the participant view shows it as the generated route. If it has no such route, the participant view shows no route and asks the group to retry or adjust the settings. A whole solve can take up to twice this limit, and you can always press Stop solving if the wait is not worth it.
Cached answers
Identical settings return the earlier answer instantly instead of solving again, which is usually what you want — but the cache is keyed on the request, so restarting the solver with different settings does not change it. Every participant then keeps getting answers from before the restart with nothing on screen to say so, and a page reload does not help.
