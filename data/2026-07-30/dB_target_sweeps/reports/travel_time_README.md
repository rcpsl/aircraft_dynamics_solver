# Travel-time supplementary dataset

`travel_time_summary.csv` — multiplier-weighted average (plus unweighted
min/max) per-aircraft travel time for every scenario CSV in `full_routes/`,
derived from the routing tables already present there. Generated by
`../compute_travel_time.py`.

**Coverage**: 127 rows, one per existing routing-enriched CSV (all files
currently present as of generation; the 5 still-missing files documented in
`../REPORT.md` §4 just aren't included — there's no routing table to
compute travel time from until those solve).

**Columns**: `file, combo, domain, mode, knob, N, totalTraffic, T_used,
avgTravelTimeWeighted, minTravelTime, maxTravelTime` — `knob` is whichever
scenario-specific label applies (target dB, D cap, or prioritarian lens).

## Definitions

**Per-aircraft travel time** = the timestep index (0-indexed, t=0 is always
Start) at which an aircraft's routed path first reaches its End station.
Aircraft with Start==End get 0. This counts any time spent parked before
departure too — it's the arrival tick, not pure time-in-motion. (If pure
time-in-motion is wanted instead, see the `_active_output_matrices`
`t_fm`/`t_arr` logic in `solver_server_dB.py` — a different, also-defensible
definition, not implemented here.)

**`avgTravelTimeWeighted`** = the **multiplier-weighted** mean of those
per-aircraft times: `sum(time_i · mult_i) / sum(mult_i)`. Each physical
aircraft represents `mult` virtual aircraft / traffic units, so this answers
*"average trip length across all the traffic served"* — consistent with how
the rest of this dataset counts aircraft (the noise CSVs report
multiplier-weighted, not physical, counts; e.g. Peak Aircraft 29 from only
14 physical aircraft). `totalTraffic` (= Σ multipliers, the weighting
denominator) is included so the figure is traceable.

**`minTravelTime` / `maxTravelTime`** = the unweighted range of per-aircraft
times. Weighting doesn't change which values are the extremes, so these are
left unweighted as a plain "shortest / longest trip any aircraft made."

## Discoveries (from the 127-file summary)

These fell out of computing travel time across the whole dataset. They're
derived purely from the routing tables, and several corroborate the noise
domain's own findings from an independent angle.

1. **A hard travel-time floor of 3 — the scheduling twin of the +3 dB noise
   floor.** No aircraft in any of the 127 files ever arrives before timestep
   3 (`minTravelTime` is only ever 3 or 4, never less). Every trip is between
   stations 1 and 16, whose unique shortest path is the 3-hop
   `1→6→11→16` — so 3 is the structural minimum, exactly the way the noise
   data bottoms out at +3.0103 dB because every route must cross a 55 dB
   bridge station (`../REPORT.md`, `equal_allocation/README.md` §9). Same
   bottleneck graph, two different floors.

2. **`minTravelTime` is a "did any traffic use the shortest corridor?"
   flag.** `minTravelTime==3` means at least one aircraft took the direct
   `1→6→11→16` path; `minTravelTime==4` means **no** aircraft did — the whole
   fleet was pushed off the shortest corridor. That full-detour case happens
   in **44 of 127 files**, heavily concentrated in prioritarian (24 of the
   44).

3. **Protecting the bottleneck community costs the whole fleet exactly one
   timestep — and it's visible as a clean 3.0 ↔ 4.0 split.** In prioritarian,
   the three lenses whose protected set includes **station 6** (the shortest
   path's middle hop) — `low_income` {6,12}, `transit_desert` {6,12,15},
   `few_jobs` {4,6,12,14} — force every aircraft off `1→6→11→16`, driving
   `avgTravelTimeWeighted` to exactly **4.0**. The one lens that does *not*
   protect station 6 — `mostly_renters` {1,3,5,10,15,16} — leaves all traffic
   on the shortest path, so it stays at **3.0**. This is the fairness↔service
   tradeoff made concrete: protecting the corridor's bottleneck community
   buys lower noise there at a flat +1-timestep travel cost for the entire
   fleet. It corroborates `prioritarian/REPORT.md` §5c ("`mostly_renters`
   diverges the most from the others") from the travel-time side — the same
   lens stands out here, for the same structural reason.

4. **Weighted travel time never exceeds 5.0, and the spread is narrow
   (3.00–5.00 across all 127).** The domains that spend the most travel time
   are the two utilitarian ones (means ~4.1) — expected, since their whole
   point is trading route length against noise/hub-congestion — while
   prioritarian and proportional cluster tightly near 3.6–3.8 under their
   `T_max=5` horizon. `T_used` is 5 for exactly the 44 prioritarian +
   proportional files and 7 for the other 83, matching each domain's
   configured `T_max`.

## Verification

`s2-55_s6-55/utilitarian_peak/weighted/result.csv` (a file with mixed
multipliers and mixed route lengths) — recomputing the weighted average by
hand from its raw routing table gives **3.768**, matching the CSV exactly,
and distinct from the unweighted mean (3.643), confirming the weighting is
applied correctly rather than collapsing to the plain average.

For a uniform-route file (`s2-55_s6-55/prioritarian/community/low_income.csv`,
where all 14 aircraft take the same 5-step route `1→2→7→11→16` arriving at
t=4), weighted and unweighted both correctly read `4.0`.

`compute_travel_time.py` is idempotent and safe to re-run once the remaining
5 files land — it'll just pick them up.
